# Best Bottles homepage artwork standard

## Purpose

Use this standard for editorial collection art and process illustrations. Product identity must remain recognizable while the rendering feels tactile, composed, and human-made.

## Generation

- Model: Higgsfield GPT Image 2.5
- Variant: Sunburst
- Output: 4K, opaque
- Homepage feature ratio: 27:16
- Use at least one verified product image as the first reference.
- Use the approved editorial artwork only as a style reference.

## Product truth

- The first product reference controls silhouette, height-to-width ratio, shoulders, base, neck, threads, closure, dispenser, and component fit.
- Do not synthesize a hybrid from several product families.
- Repeat a bottle only when every copy has the same geometry.
- Detached components must fit the visible neck and form a physically possible assembly.
- Keep important product details unobscured and compare the final artwork with the source before use.

## Visual language

- Warm ivory archival paper
- Fine graphite contours and visible crosshatching
- Convincing clear-glass transparency and soft contact shadows
- Selective cobalt-blue and antique-gold accents
- A composed editorial still life with modest overlap and generous negative space
- The artwork fills its canvas; do not place a narrow white canvas inside the image

## Avoid

- Stone-platform repetition
- Photorealistic cutouts placed into a sketch
- Vague foliage or decorative background filler
- Warped glass, drifting proportions, invented closures, extra bottles, labels, logos, or text
- Floating parts or impossible assemblies

## Review checklist

1. Compare the generated silhouette with the first product reference.
2. Check body width, shoulder shape, base thickness, neck threads, and closure fit.
3. Confirm the scene contains only the requested components.
4. Inspect the actual homepage crop on desktop and mobile.
5. Treat visual approval as separate from catalog or production publication.
