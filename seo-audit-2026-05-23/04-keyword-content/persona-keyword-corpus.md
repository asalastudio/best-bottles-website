# Best Bottles — Persona-Mapped Keyword Corpus (Stage 4a)

**Audit lead:** Asala Studio · Jordan Richter (jordan@asala.ai)
**Date:** 2026-05-23
**Target launch:** 2026-06-15
**Scope:** A B2B keyword corpus for the new `bestbottles.com`, organized around the five buyer personas Best Bottles is selling to. Each persona gets a snapshot, ~30 high-intent keywords spanning the buyer journey (research → spec → sample → quote → reorder), intent and difficulty calls, and the landing-page type each keyword should resolve to.

This document is the input to Stage 4b (content roadmap, Weeks 13-26) and Stage 4c (PDP + category copy templates). It does NOT duplicate keyword work covered in `docs/SEO_CONTENT_CALENDAR.md` (Weeks 1-12) — those keywords are referenced where they cluster but treated as already-claimed.

Total corpus: 150 keywords across 5 personas, 30 keywords per persona.

---

## Read-out before the corpus

Three patterns held across every persona we mapped, and they shape the entire corpus:

1. **B2B intent fragments into 5 stages, not Google's 4.** Generic SEO frameworks (informational / commercial / transactional / navigational) collapse the middle of the funnel. In glass packaging the middle is where the work is: a buyer can be in **research** ("what is a 24-410 neck finish"), **spec** ("amber boston round 30ml dropper"), **sample** ("free packaging sample kit"), **quote** ("USA glass bottle wholesale Net 30"), or **reorder** ("boston round 4oz amber case quantity") and each stage needs a different landing page type. The corpus uses these five stages explicitly.

2. **Difficulty is bimodal in this category.** Generic head terms ("wholesale glass bottles") are crowded with Berlin Packaging, Specialty Bottle, SKS, Container & Packaging — high difficulty, high volume, slow payoff. Long-tail spec queries ("24-410 amber boston round dropper bottle wholesale 30ml") are nearly empty SERPs — low difficulty, low volume, fast payoff with strong commercial intent. The corpus weights heavily toward long-tail because that is where Best Bottles can rank inside 90 days.

3. **Persona ≠ keyword cluster, but persona × buyer stage = keyword cluster.** An indie founder at research stage searches differently from an indie founder at quote stage. The corpus organizes inside each persona by stage to make this explicit, so the content roadmap (Stage 4b) can build the right page for the right moment.

## Persona 2 — Established Brand Procurement Manager

**Snapshot.** Mid-career procurement, supply-chain, or operations manager at a $5M-$100M cosmetics, personal-care, or fragrance brand. Job title is "Sourcing Manager", "Procurement Manager", "Packaging Manager", or "Supply Chain Lead". Reports to a VP of Operations. Owns the bottle, cap, and applicator spend across an entire product line. Buys on a PO, requires Net 30 or Net 45, needs spec sheets, certs of analysis, and a documented supplier-quality program. Pain points: their incumbent supplier just raised prices 18%; their R&D team launched a new SKU and Procurement is the bottleneck; they need a backup supplier in case the primary has a freight delay. Watering holes: ISM (Institute for Supply Management), LinkedIn procurement communities, ThomasNet, Packaging Digest, Beauty Packaging magazine, Cosmoprof exhibitor lists, Faire Wholesale Trends report.

Primary search behavior: specific, formal, and spec-led. Searches with company names, certifications (FDA, ISO 9001), and payment-term terms (Net 30). High intent on first search; very few research-stage searches. Best Bottles' current calendar (Weeks 2, 4, 9) starts to court this persona but does not own the procurement-document keyword cluster.

## Persona 3 — Contract Filler / Co-Packer Buyer

**Snapshot.** Operations or sourcing lead at a contract manufacturer that fills bottles for client beauty/fragrance/wellness brands. Title is "Operations Manager", "Sourcing Lead", "Account Manager", or "Component Buyer". Companies range from 10-person regional fillers to 200-person national co-packers. Buys bottles by the pallet and case, not by the piece. Re-buys the same SKUs every month for ongoing client production runs and needs new SKUs spec'd inside two weeks when a client launches. Pain points: cap-bottle fitment failure on the filling line, lead-time slippage, freight allocation across multiple client jobs, sudden client demand spikes (Q4 holiday gifting, Mother's Day, Black Friday). Watering holes: PMMI (Packaging Machinery Manufacturers Institute), Pack Expo, Contract Packaging Association (CPA), Packaging Digest, Contract Pharma, Cosmoprof.

Primary search behavior: volume-oriented, technical, and lead-time-aware. Buys what the client specs, but also recommends bottles when the client is flexible. Treats the bottle supplier like a manufacturing input — wants to know case quantities, pallet counts, freight class, NMFC codes, and CCT (corked-cap-tested) certifications.

## Persona 4 — Cosmetic Formulator

**Snapshot.** R&D chemist or independent cosmetic formulator. Title is "Cosmetic Chemist", "Formulator", "R&D Manager", "Product Development Manager", or "Lab Director". Works at a CPG brand, an independent lab (Belmar Labs, Cosmewax, Lubrizol contract formulation), or as a freelance formulator (Perry Romanowski's "Chemists Corner" community is a strong indicator). Spec's neck finishes, materials, capacities, and dispensing systems for new formulations. Cares about chemical resistance, sterilization compatibility, headspace volume, light transmission percentages, and migration risk. Pain points: a formula that performed in glass pilot batches behaves differently in PET production scale-up; the dispenser the marketing team picked doesn't match the viscosity of the final formula; the bottle the procurement team sourced doesn't meet the SPF stability requirements. Watering holes: Society of Cosmetic Chemists (SCC), Cosmetics & Toiletries magazine, Personal Care Products Council, in-cosmetics conference, Chemists Corner forum, CosmeticsBusiness, Happi.

Primary search behavior: technical, abbreviation-heavy, and references industry standards. Searches like a chemist — "PET vs glass for serum packaging" not "what bottle for my serum". ## Cross-persona patterns

Three patterns surfaced when comparing the five personas:

### Pattern 1 — The "low MOQ" claim is multi-persona ammo

Keywords 6-9 of Persona 1 (indie), keywords 19-22 of Persona 3 (filler), and keywords 11-15 of Persona 5 (designer) all touch MOQ. This is the single positioning wedge Specialty Bottle owns today (Stage 3 §10.4). Best Bottles' ~$50 minimum is a far stronger MOQ posture than the indie and designer personas know. A single category-level page (`/collections/low-moq-glass-bottles`) plus FAQ block on every PDP listing the per-SKU minimum would feed all three personas.

### Pattern 2 — Sample-kit content is undermonetized

Persona 1 keywords 17-19, Persona 2 keyword 26, Persona 3 keyword 24, Persona 4 keywords 13-14, and Persona 5 keyword 27 all point at sample-stage intent. The existing calendar (Post 10, Post 19) addresses this twice but does not own the explicit "free packaging sample" / "sample pack" / "sample kit" keyword cluster. A `/request-sample` page already exists (Stage 0 brief, §URL structure table) but is not built for SEO. Stage 4b adds a Week 13 pillar "How do I order a sample kit from Best Bottles?" specifically to make this page rank.

### Pattern 3 — Brand reference keywords (Persona 5 #7-#10) are a backdoor B2B link play

"Aesop style bottle wholesale alternative" / "Le Labo style apothecary bottle wholesale" are low-difficulty, low-volume keywords that brand designers screenshot and share inside design briefs. Each blog post in this cluster can earn organic links from designer Slack groups, Dribbble shots, and Are.na blocks — all of which AI engines crawl. Even at low volume, the link equity compounds.

### Pattern 4 — The procurement persona is invisible in the existing calendar

Persona 2 has 30 high-intent keywords and zero existing-calendar posts written explicitly for the reader. This is the single largest unaddressed segment in Best Bottles' current content strategy and the highest dollar value per visitor.
